# Live

## Live detection

- A watched channel has "watch lives" on and at least one enabled tab source.
- Every minute, per watched channel, the scheduler reads three places: the RSS feed, the first page of the Live tab, and the `/live` page.
- The RSS feed holds only the 15 newest uploads. On a busy channel a waiting room drops out of it days before the stream, so the Live tab's `is_upcoming` and `is_live` entries are candidates too.
- Each candidate id is probed with yt-dlp and `ignore_no_formats_error`. Without that option an upcoming stream raises "This live event will begin in ..." instead of returning `release_timestamp`. The Live tab listing and the probes use the general cookie profile. The RSS feed takes no cookies. A probe that fails with a members-only error is probed once more with the channel's first member profile by name, if one is present. A probe that still fails, such as a private video or a members-only video no profile may watch, is skipped.
- A premiere is not recorded. yt-dlp gives an upcoming or running premiere the `live_status` of a stream, so detection reads `media_type`: `livestream` for a stream, `video` for a premiere. A skipped premiere logs `live.skipped` with `reason=premiere`. Once it ends, the archive poll treats it as an upload. A `live_seen` row without `media_type` counts as a stream.
- A probed id is a `live_seen` row with the probe's fields, or null for a probe that failed (storage.md). A check reads the rows of its candidate ids in one query and probes only ids without a row, so a scheduler restart probes nothing already seen. A row with the probe's fields is never probed again. A null row is probed again 24 hours after its `probed_at`, because a member profile or a membership may have appeared. A success stores the fields, and another refusal sets `probed_at` to the check's time.
- One upsert per check stores the new rows and sets `listed_at` on every candidate id. The upsert runs also when a probe cuts the check off, so the probes before it stay stored. The hourly database cleanup deletes a row 30 days after its `listed_at`. A check touches every id it lists, so only an id gone from the feed and the Live tab for 30 days is deleted.
- A detection through the member profile, or whose probe reports `availability` `subscriber_only`, stores its video as type `members`. Its recording then takes a member profile (access.md, Cookies and account).
- A members-only detection of a channel with `members_all` on passes the title words of any watching source (archive.md, Title words).
- Every detection of a channel with `keep_all` on passes the title words of any watching source.
- The `/live` page probe takes the general profile only.
- The `/live` page finds a stream with no waiting room. "The channel is not currently live" is the normal answer and is not an error.
- No Data API key is used. The probe reads the scheduled start that `videos.list` would.
- A detection is a queued `record` job. `scheduled_at` comes from `release_timestamp` for an upcoming stream and is null for a stream found live. The record job keeps a video to one detection. A video the user deleted gets no record job (storage.md, Deleting a video).
- The first check of a newly watched channel probes up to 15 feed ids and takes about a minute. Later checks take seconds.
- A queued record job does not stop the archive poll from queuing the VOD. The poll skips a live until it ends, so the VOD is queued only when the recording has not stored the video.
- WebSub push needs a public HTTPS callback, which the tailnet-only cluster lacks. Not used.

## Recording and the VOD

- Every 15 s the scheduler starts a recorder Job for each queued record job due within 5 minutes: `scheduled_at` within 5 minutes, already past, or null for a stream found live. The recorder runs `savetube record --job-id <id>` with `wait_for_video` and `live_from_start` (`backend/savetube/recorders.py`).
- The scheduler lists Jobs by the `savetube.io/job-id` label, so a job with a Job, finished or not, never gets a second. A recorder Job that spent its `backoffLimit` while its row is still queued or running marks the row `missed` with `recorder_failed` and the video `failed`.
- The loop runs only inside a cluster, where `KUBERNETES_SERVICE_HOST` is set. The compose scheduler makes no recorder Jobs.
- A recording skips the `live_chat` track. yt-dlp writes subtitles before the media, and a running live's chat downloads until the stream ends. The VOD's archive keeps the chat replay.
- A recording writes its progress to `job.progress` every 5 s, as an archive download does, through the same pump on a thread instead of a child process. The video and audio streams download at once, so `done` and `speed` sum over the streams. A live has no total, so `pct` and `total` stay null. The recorder sends no format pin: it serves the archive resume, and a VOD queued later must not take a live format. It sends each stream's size when that stream finishes, and the stored `info` carries it as the stream's `filesize`.
- A finished recording gets the probe and packets checks and its audio copy (archive.md, Resuming and verifying downloads, Audio copy), then moves straight into the tree under the video's `lives` or `members` directory and marks the video complete. A failed check keeps it, unverified.
- Some streams get no VOD after they end. For those, the recording is the only copy and stays.

## Format watch

YouTube serves a live's VOD first in H.264 and AAC-or-Opus, and adds VP9 or AV1 at full resolution hours or days later, if ever. A live stream serves only AAC audio (`140`), while the VOD serves Opus (`251`) from the start. The user keeps lives mostly for the audio.

- A live video gets a format watch for 7 days from when it aired, once it turns `complete`: a finished recording, or a VOD download of a live (`was_live`). Members-only lives are included. A live that aired 7 or more days before it turns `complete`, such as a past live a backfill downloads, gets no watch, because YouTube adds no formats to an old VOD.
- The air time is the info dict's `release_timestamp`, else its `timestamp`, else `video.upload_date` at midnight UTC (`savetube.watch.aired_at`). A live with none of them gets no watch.
- Every 6 hours the scheduler probes each watched video's formats, with the general profile, or the member profile for type `members`. It stores the format list and the VOD duration.
- Only `https` formats count. HLS (`m3u8`) copies of the same stream are ignored. Audio formats with `-drc` in the id are ignored, because dynamic range compression flattens singing.
- Audio pick: Opus over AAC, then the higher `abr`.
- Video pick: among formats with height and fps at least the stored stream's, AV1 over VP9 over H.264, then the higher `tbr` within one codec. Bitrates of different codecs are not compared. A stored Premium format (`format_note` has "Premium", or itag 616) is never replaced.
- A format with the stored stream's format id never ranks above it, audio or video. One itag is one stream, and its listed bitrate differs between clients.
- A pick that ranks above the stored stream queues a replace job. Audio and video are judged apart: a replace downloads only the stream that changes and remuxes it with the stored other stream through `ffmpeg -c copy`.
- Before any replace, the VOD duration is compared with the stored file's `ffprobe` duration. A difference over 2 s holds the replace and the UI shows it with the difference in seconds. The 2 s is fixed, not the 1% of the download check.
- A replace passes the same integrity checks as a download, then swaps the file in the tree and writes a new audio copy.
- In an open watch, the first stored file, the recording or the first VOD download, moves aside and stays until the watch ends, then is deleted. A swap outside an open watch keeps the file it replaced, or the original already aside, until 7 days after that swap (`format_watch.original_until`), then the format watch loop deletes it. A file from an intermediate replace is deleted at the next replace.
- The video page lists the stored file and every counted YouTube format: codec, resolution and fps, bitrate, size, duration, and the duration difference. A stored stream's size is its `filesize` in `info`, else its `filesize_approx`, else bitrate × stored duration marked as an estimate. A stored stream whose `protocol` is `https` is a YouTube stream: a VOD download, or a stream a replace swapped in. When the probe lists its format id, that format's row stands for it, marked stored, so it is not listed twice. A recorded stream (`http_dash_segments_generator`), or a YouTube stream the probe does not list, gets its own stored row. The formats come best first. Video: height, then fps, then AV1 over VP9 over H.264, then `tbr`. Audio: Opus over AAC, then `abr`. The pick carries a `recommended` badge with a one-line reason.
- The user can pick one listed video format and one listed audio format there, in one replace job, a held video included after a confirm, also after the watch ends. A format with a recorded stream's id can be picked: a live recording and its VOD share an itag but are different encodes. The row of a stored YouTube stream keeps it, and the API refuses its id. A manual pick ends the automatic replaces for that video. It cancels an automatic replace still queued, with its queue job, and logs `job.cancelled`. A running replace, or a queued manual one, refuses the pick.
- "Probe now" on the video page probes any `complete` video's formats at once, live or not, through the scheduler's API (runtime.md, Scheduler API). It uses the watch's profile rule. On an open watch it counts as the watch's probe and may queue a replace. On an ended watch it only refreshes the formats, and the watch stays ended. A video without a row gets one with a null `started_at`: no automatic watch, so the loop never probes it or queues a replace for it. The user's probes and the loop's take one lock, so one probe runs at a time.
- A video with no VOD keeps its recording. The watch ends without a replace.
- A watch is a `format_watch` row (storage.md). It starts when a recording or a live's VOD is stored `complete`, or when a staged VOD moves into the tree. `started_at` holds the live's air time, not that moment.
- The scheduler's format watch loop runs every 60 s (`savetube.watch`). It ends each watch past 7 days that has no replace job queued or running, deletes each original past its `original_until` with no replace queued or running, then probes each watch whose last probe is 6 hours old, one at a time. A failed probe stores its error and waits 6 hours like a success.
- The stored stream comes from the video's `info.requested_formats`. The stored duration is read once, and again after each replace.
- A replace is a `job` of kind `replace`, with the format ids it downloads in `job.format_id`. One job carries every stream that changes. A watch with a replace queued or running gets no second.
- The worker runs a replace job through the archive queue, under the download limits (archive.md, Download limits). The dispatcher starts a picked replace (`job.manual`) before any waiting archive job, because the user waits on the video page. An automatic replace starts only when no archive job waits, so new downloads go first. It downloads the format alone, checks it as an archive download is checked, remuxes it with the stored file's other stream into an mkv, and checks the result with the probe and the packet pass. A pause or a worker stop puts it back to waiting, and a YouTube block interrupts it as it does an archive job. Any other failure fails it and leaves the stored file alone. The next probe may queue another.
- The swap hard-links the stored file into `.originals/<video id>/` before the remux renames over it, when no original is kept yet, so the tree never lacks the file. The old audio copy is deleted and a new one written. `video.info`, its `.info.json`, `format_id` and `size_bytes` take the new streams, the downloaded one with its measured size as `filesize`.
- Log lines: `watch.probed`, `watch.queued`, `watch.held`, `watch.probe_failed`, `watch.probe_requested`, `watch.probe_request_failed` (web), `watch.ended`, `watch.original_deleted`, `watch.picked`, `job.cancelled` (web, a pick cancels a queued automatic replace), `replace.start`, `replace.swapped`, `replace.done`.
